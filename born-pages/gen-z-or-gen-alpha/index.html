Gen Z or Gen Alpha?

Enter a birth year to compare two commonly cited generation systems and understand why 2010–2012 can receive different labels.

Last reviewed: July 28, 2026

Quick answer

There is no universal Gen Z–Gen Alpha cutoff. A Library of Congress research guide presents Gen Z as 1997–2012 and notes that Generation Alpha may begin as early as 2010. McCrindle uses Gen Z 1995–2009 and Generation Alpha 2010–2024. As a result, people born in 2010, 2011 or 2012 may be classified as Gen Z by one system and Gen Alpha by another.

Why do the dates overlap?

  • Generation labels are analytical and cultural groupings, not legal classifications.
  • Different researchers use different historical events, technologies and cohort lengths.
  • Generation Alpha is still young, so its final boundaries are less settled than the Baby Boom dates.
  • A source may describe Alpha as beginning “as early as 2010” without requiring every other generation chart to use that exact year.

For someone born near the boundary, the most accurate answer is often to name the source: “Gen Z under the 1997–2012 range, but Gen Alpha under McCrindle's 2010–2024 definition.”

Frequently asked questions

Is 2010 Gen Z or Gen Alpha?

It depends on the source. The 1997–2012 Gen Z range classifies 2010 as Gen Z, while McCrindle's 2010–2024 definition classifies it as Gen Alpha.

Is 2011 Gen Z or Gen Alpha?

2011 is Gen Z under a range ending in 2012 and Gen Alpha under a range beginning in 2010.

Is 2012 Gen Z or Gen Alpha?

The Library of Congress guide presents 2012 as the final Gen Z year. McCrindle includes 2012 in Generation Alpha.

Is 2013 Gen Alpha?

Yes under both systems compared on this page: it follows the 1997–2012 Gen Z range and also falls within McCrindle's 2010–2024 Alpha range.

Which range does QuickAgeCalc use?

The site's main generation calculator uses Gen Z 1997–2012 and Gen Alpha from 2013 so the ranges do not overlap. The focused checker on this page also shows the alternative McCrindle result.

Do generation labels determine personality?

No. They describe broad cohorts and cannot determine an individual's identity, values, skills or behavior.
